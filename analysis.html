Analysis

What they do, why it works, and what is worth taking.

Read this first. This is not a webinar funnel and there is nothing to transcribe. There is no VSL, no evergreen class, no replay page and no recorded pitch — the only video assets are short Framer background loops and Vimeo course modules behind the paid wall. The $10K Brand Deal Challenge runs live Aug 17–20 2026, so the pitch does not exist yet. Everything below is read off 15 captured pages, a live checkout, and the raw config of the live application form.

Why this one is in the file

Justin Moore runs the opposite machine and appears to make it work — $5M+ in personal sponsorship revenue, a published book, Ali Abdaal as a public client, and a $12,000 program with no ad spend behind it.

He is not a direct competitor. He sells to creators who already get inbound brand interest; we sell to people trying to become creators. But he is selling into the same room, and three of his mechanics attack problems we actually have.

Worth taking

Make the guarantee conditional on attendance

“Full refund — if you attend all the live sessions with your camera on.” The refund is only claimable by someone who showed up to everything. Show rate is our number-one bottleneck and we currently attack it with follow-up volume. He attacks it with the terms of the offer, and it costs nothing to run.

Charge $27 instead of charging nothing

A paid front end buys a card on file, a phone number, and legal SMS consent — then the $590 bonus stack makes the $27 feel like theft. A $27–47 paid front end would filter the tyre-kickers our setters currently burn hours on and give us a callable list of proven buyers.

Put the price in the application as a commitment question

“Would it be well worth the $12k investment to work with us? Yes / No / Maybe”, followed by “how soon would you want to get started?” The prospect types the price and the timeline before a closer ever dials. Both objections are pre-handled in writing, and the No/Maybe answers are a free routing signal.

Branch the application on proof of money

If they have closed deals recently he asks for revenue bands. If they haven’t, he asks “have you invested money into growing your business in the last 12 months?” Two different definitions of qualified, one form. Our lead grader already weights proven-earner and ability-to-pay heaviest — this is the same logic expressed as branching form logic instead of a scoring rubric.

Make the lead magnet a recurring deliverable

Not a PDF. An actual list of sponsorship opportunities, twice a week, forever. It is the reason 27,000 subscribers is enough traffic to carry a business with no ad spend. Our equivalent would be a recurring feed of live brand briefs the audience genuinely can’t get elsewhere.

Give a live diagnosis on a real stranger

Day 3 picks creators live, takes their dream brand, and decodes that brand’s social in real time. It cannot be scripted, it proves the mechanism better than any testimonial, and it makes that day unskippable.

Name the enemy in the nav

“0% Cut Policy — we’re coaches, not managers.” Top nav and footer of every page. He is not arguing against another course, he is arguing against a talent manager taking 20%. One line, on every page, and every visitor knows what he is instead of what he does.

Let the prize be the product

Seven attendees at Sponsor Games win real $2,500 brand deals, funded by his own sponsors. The event demonstrates the mechanism and someone else pays for the demonstration.

Not worth taking

The no-ads model is not portable to us. He spent 15 years building the audience that feeds this, and the book plus the Ali Abdaal relationship are not assets you can buy this quarter. Reading “he runs no ads” as a strategy would be the wrong lesson — the lesson is what a paid front end does for a list, and that works with paid traffic too.

No guarantee on the $12k. He can get away with that because the book, the podcast and the named clients have already done the trust work. We cannot, and our objection set is different — portfolio-perfectionism and burned-by-a-past-coach both need a guarantee to answer them.

The price-hidden coaching page. wizardsguild.com and creatorwizard.com/coaching both 301 straight to the application form, so a warm visitor who wants to evaluate the offer has nowhere to land. That is a leak, not a technique.

Open questions — not captured

  • What happens between the $27 buy and the application. The challenge doesn’t start until Aug 17 and the checkout requires a card and a phone number, so under the swipe hard rules we never submitted. No post-purchase sequence, no order bump, no upsell path, and no evidence of how Day 4 pitches into the coaching.
  • The paid-traffic application. The live form is titled “(Organic)”, which means a second version exists for paid traffic. We have not found it, and its existence is the strongest single hint that he does run ads somewhere we haven’t looked.
  • Whether $12,000 is the only price. The application says $12k and the affiliates page says “5 figures”. A 2024 third-party write-up described $297/mo group and $1k/mo private tiers — almost certainly retired, but there is no current price page to check against.
  • Email sequences. Not yet in the inbox. Registering the research identity for the newsletter would surface his welcome flow and the challenge promo sequence — a one-line opt-in with no phone field, so it is safe to run if Will wants it.
